# Problem statement

## Who is affected

Port shift supervisors, berth planners, yard coordinators, and vessel
operations teams need a shared view of what will happen during the next shift.
They must balance vessel ETA/ETD commitments against finite berth length,
draft, crane, and yard capacity.

## Why current workflows fall short

Spreadsheets and separate operational tools show individual facts but do not
connect incoming vessel volume to berth sequencing, yard pressure, disruptions,
and downstream rerouting decisions. This forces supervisors to manually
reconcile timelines and makes emerging congestion easy to miss.

## Why it matters now

Weather events and equipment breakdowns can change arrival pressure quickly.
Even a small amount of avoidable waiting creates knock-on effects for berth
utilization, yard moves, and customer commitments. A transparent decision
support layer can surface the next intervention without pretending that
synthetic demo data is production history.
